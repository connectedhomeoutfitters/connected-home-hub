Getting paid

Card payments, into your account

ConnectedWorkOS collects card payments through a payment processor you connect yourself. Today that processor is Stripe. Your money goes to your bank, we never hold it, and card numbers never touch our servers.

Setting it up

Connecting Stripe, step by step

This is a one-time job and takes about ten minutes, most of it Stripe's own onboarding rather than ours. You'll want your business details and a bank account to hand.

  1. 01

    Open Settings → Payments

    Inside ConnectedWorkOS. There's one button: Connect with Stripe.

  2. 02

    Sign in to Stripe, or create an account

    If you already take card payments through Stripe, sign in and pick that account — you don't need a second one. If you don't have one, you create it here, and it's free to open.

  3. 03

    Give Stripe your business details

    Stripe asks the questions a payment processor is legally required to ask: your business name and structure, an address, your EIN or SSN for a sole proprietor, and the bank account for payouts. This is between you and Stripe — we never see it.

  4. 04

    You land back in ConnectedWorkOS, connected

    That's it. Quotes can now take a deposit on acceptance, and invoices can be paid by card.

Until you connect an account, you cannot take card payments. Quoting, scheduling and invoicing all still work, but the pay page will tell your customer that payment isn't available. The setup checklist on your dashboard flags this for exactly that reason.

Where the money goes

It is your account, not ours

The connection is a Stripe Connect authorisation: you own the account, you agree to Stripe's terms directly with Stripe, and we're permitted to create charges on your behalf. We never take custody of your money, and we never hold your keys.

What it costs

Two separate bills, and we only send one

From us

Your subscription

A flat monthly or annual price for ConnectedWorkOS, billed as part of your Connected Home Ledger plan. It does not change with how much you invoice — we take no percentage of your jobs.

From Stripe

Card processing fees

Stripe charges a per-transaction fee on each card payment and deducts it before payout. Those rates are Stripe's, they're published on Stripe's pricing page, and they can change — so we don't quote a number here that might be wrong by the time you read it.

Nobody takes a cut of your revenue except the card networks. Some field-service tools charge you a percentage of every invoice on top of processing. We don't — you pay a subscription, Stripe charges for moving money, and that's the whole list.

Day to day

Once it's connected

Deposits

Collected on acceptance

Set a deposit percentage per quote. Accepting it raises the invoice and emails the pay link in the same action.

Balances

Billed when the job's done

Marking the work complete raises the remaining balance, net of the deposit. Send it, or let it sit until you're ready.

Refunds

Full or partial

Refund from the payment it belongs to. The customer gets a receipt, and your books are adjusted so the income isn't overstated.

Reconciliation

You can see the money

Every payment, with card brand and last four, a link to the Stripe receipt, and a filterable sales journal you can export as CSV for your accountant.

Security

Card data never reaches us

The payment form is Stripe's, embedded in the page. Card numbers go from your customer to Stripe directly — our servers never see or store them.

Your customer

No account required

They click the link in your email, see the invoice, and pay. There's nothing to sign up for and nothing to install.

Other processors
